**Step 1: Receive Points**
Each character receives 65 Building Points, 15 Learning Points, 15 Advancement Points, and 6 Racial Points for character creation. These starting amounts may be further modified by his or her ability scores (and detailed background – if you take the time to pursue this additional step).

**Step 2: Roll Ability Scores**
Each character has seven ability scores that describe him. To determine your character’s ability scores, roll 48d6 and tally the totals of each individual number. Add 3 to each of these totals, but if any of the totals are above 18, then re-roll all excess dice until the sum is 18 exactly. Assign the six results to Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, Constitution, and Charisma in any order you wish, then roll a 3d6 for Looks. You’ll also need to roll d% (represented by a d100 or two d10s of different colors, one each for 10s and 1s) for the fractional ability of each. This number determines how close the character is to reaching the next ability score. Once a stat increases beyond “00”, the stat improves by one integer. Thus, adding 5 to a 14/95 Strength score results in a 14/00 Strength, while adding 6 results in a 15/01 Strength. Do this process twice, and choose which distribution you prefer for your character.

**Step 3: Choose A Race**
Select your character’s race.

**Step 4: Choose An Alignment**
Select your character's alignment.

**Step 5: Finalize Ability Scores**
You may increase your ability scores at a rate of 0/05 per Building Point (e.g. 20 BPs raise an ability score by a full point). Characters with scores below 10/01 may increase them at a rate of 0/10 per BP until they reach 10/01. Note that scores of 16/01 or greater receive only 0/03 per BP.

Now it’s time to tabulate all adjustments to your
ability scores. These may be due to the race you’ve
chosen or from spending Building Points (above). Note that your Looks ability score may modify
your Charisma.
You may adjust your ability scores after this step by
spending additional BPs but such increases will not
count towards your starting Honor.

**Step 6: Calculate Starting Honor**
Now add the seven ability scores from Step 5 together
(including fractionals) and divide that sum by seven,
disregarding any fractions in the result. If necessary,
adjust the result by any Honor modifiers from your
Charisma and Looks scores, background, or quirks and
flaws. This is your starting Honor. Record the result on
your character sheet.

**Step 7: Determine Priors and Particulars**
In this step you will determine your height, weight, age, handedness and some background information about your character.

**Step 8: Determine Quirks and Flaws**
At this point, you may add quirks and flaws to your
character. Quirks and flaws are optional but offer the
opportunity to gain extra building points that may be used
to enhance your character.
Quirks include mental traits such as habits and
prejudices, while flaws are physical traits such as poor eyesight and hearing. To determine which quirks or flaws the
character has, you may either “cherry pick” individual items
(safer but less rewarding) or roll randomly (risky but with the possibility for
far greater bonus Building Points).

Step 9: Record Advancement Talents
Each character begins with the first tier of each Advancement Talent free of charge.

**Step 10: Purchase Skills, Talents and Proficiencies**
Now you can spend any remaining points on
skills, talents and/or proficiencies (STPs, for short). You
also have the option to hold BPs, LPs, APs, and RPs in reserve for future
expenditure. Be sure to check your racial talents, as some races must make a decision at first level.

**Step 11: Determine Hit Points**
Each character receives Hit Points as
follows: Racial Base HP (usually 10) + Constitution multiplied by Health Advancement value (rounded). This
total may be further modified by such things as talents,
flaws, etc. Health values can be found within the Advancement Tables handout.

**Step 12: Record Derived Statistics**
A character’s Base Initiative and Base Defense is determined by his Wisdom and Dexterity modifiers, while Base
Attack is determined by his Intelligence and Dexterity
modifiers. A character’s Base Damage is determined by his
Strength. A character's sprint duration is half his Constitution in seconds (rounded down). Don't forget to apply Advancement Talents.

**Step 13: Receive Starting Money and Purchase Gear**
Each character receives 75+4d12p iron pieces to buy appropriate equipment for your character, keeping the unspent currency. Calculate carrying capacity and encumbrance according to your Strength and the weight of your items. Worn armor and apparel does not contribute to encumbrance, unless they are containers such as backpacks, belt pouches, scabbards, etc.

Characters created mid-campaign receive 2 free encounters of weapon experience rolls and 3 free downtime activity rolls per character level.
