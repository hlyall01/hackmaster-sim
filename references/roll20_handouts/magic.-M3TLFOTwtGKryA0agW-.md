**Essence Cost:** Each type of magic has different methods of generating essence to pay for its use, and various rules around casting spells. An individual may have at most two different essences. Each essence type allows the magic user to store a maximum amount of essence equal to their highest relevant ability score for that essence, multiplied by their Essence Advancement value (rounded to nearest ten).

**Essence Proficiency and Talents:** Magic users have a proficiency level with each type of essence they possess, ranging from I to V. A proficiency of I allows the magic user to cast first level spells, and grants them the initial essence talent. A proficiency of II advances the magic user's spellcasting ability, improving the level of spell that they can cast to half their character level (rounded up), and grants them the second essence talent. A proficiency of III improves the level of spell that they can cast further, to their full character level, and grants the third essence talent. A proficiency of lV allows the caster to amplify their spells, and grants the fourth essence talent. A proficiency of V allows the caster to overamplify their spells, and grants the fifth and final essence talent. The talents are retained even if their owner no longer has access to the associated essence.

**Essence Amplification:** Many spells allow the magic user to spend more essence in order to increase the power of the spell being cast. Amplified spells are spells that have been amplified to at most 200% of the spell's base essence cost. Overamplified spells are spells that have been amplified to at most 300% of the spell's base cost. The total amount of essence that can be used when casting a spell is limited to 300% of the spell's base cost (i.e. no more than 200% over the base cost). Take note that amplified and overamplified spells may cause spell mishaps.

**Components:** Most spells require a somatic (S) and/or verbal (V) component to be cast successfully. Spells with a somatic component require gestures throughout the entire casting time, the magic user's arms must be unrestricted. Vocalizations are only required on the final second of casting, when the spell is completed, magic users tend to accompany the release of essence with a shout or other vocalization.

**Casting Time:** Once a magic user starts to cast, it takes a certain amount of time before it discharges. A magic user in the process of casting is limited to a d8p defense roll against attacks and can only move at a walking speed. If he is struck or otherwise unexpectedly interrupted, the spell is disrupted, essence spent, and the caster must make a Volatility Save (regardless if the spell was amplified or not). A magic user can choose to cancel his cast, essence unchanged, at any point during the casting time, however he will suffer Spell Fatigue for 5 seconds.

**Channeling:** Some spells must be channeled to have an effect. A magic user channeling a spell suffers the same defense and movement penalties as when casting and may still be interrupted, as above, however no further Volatility Save need be made as the essence is already spent. Spell Fatigue comes into effect once the caster stops channeling.

**Spell Fatigue:** After casting a spell, your character will
suffer the debilitating effects of exhaustion due to summoning
forth and channeling magic. This weariness is known in the
business as Spell Fatigue. Spell Fatigue causes the following ill
effects:
_• A reduction in ability to defend (-6 to defense rolls)

• Attacking is not possible

• Casting is not possible
• A 30% penalty to all skill checks

• All movement is at half speed while running and sprinting are impossible

• All other actions require twice as much time to complete_
Spell fatigue lasts for 5 seconds plus the casting time of the
spell that caused it. Should the spellcaster have a weapon in
hand, it is consider freshly drawn for purposes of determining
his next attack opportunity (i.e., once fatigue ends, the spellcaster must wait his weapon speed to attack with his melee
weapon).

**Spell Failure:** A magic user has a 25% chance of his spells failing if he is lightly encumbered, 50% if moderately, and 75% if heavily. Furthermore, an additional 25% spell failure is added if using heavy armor or a shield.

**Range:** When a spell lists a range in feet, its effects begin
within or at the end of this range, at the mage’s discretion.
Touch attacks require a normal attack roll. If the target in question chooses to use a shield against the mage, the spellcaster
merely needs to hit the shield (i.e., the spell travels through the
shield and counts as a successful touch).

**Area of Effect:** Each spell affects a certain creature,
object, volume, weight, etc., as specified by the spell and desired
by the caster.

**Duration:** All spells last for a certain amount of time, be it seconds, minutes, hours or longer. Some spell durations may even
pass so quickly that they seem to take almost no time at all.

**Magic Save:** Creatures subject to a spell may attempt a saving throw by rolling a d20p and adding their
bonus to the roll. Each spell for which a save is allowed lists whether the Physical, Mental or Dodge
bonus should be used.
The magic user rolls a d20p and adds his level. If the target’s score equals or exceeds the caster’s score, he succeeds at the saving throw and the spell has no effect on
him (unless otherwise specified in the description).
A natural “1” result on any saving throw indicates failure, unless the mage himself rolls a “1” on the opposed
die check.

**Damage:** Unless otherwise stated, all damaging spells ignore Damage Reduction from shields and armor and cause no knock-back.

**Spell Dismissal:** A magic user may voluntarily terminate any spell he has cast by performing a somatic gesture.
